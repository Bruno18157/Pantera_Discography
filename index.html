Metal Magic (1983)

Release Date: June 10, 1983

Metal Magic is the debut studio album by Pantera, released on June 10, 1983, through Metal Magic Records. Like the band's next three releases, it is musically oriented toward a glam/heavy metal sound influenced by Kiss and Van Halen. The album was released on the band's own label (also called Metal Magic) and produced by Jerry Abbott, a country music songwriter and producer and the father of Vinnie Paul and Dimebag Darrell (then known as Diamond Darrell), who were 19 and 16 years old, respectively, at the time of release.

Projects in the Jungle (1984)

Release Date: July 27, 1984

Projects in the Jungle is the second studio by Pantera, released on July 27, 1984, through Metal Magic Records. The band would make their first music video for the track "All Over Tonight". Though the album, particularly Glaze's vocals, shares many similarities with Def Leppard's pre-Hysteria sound (Pyromania had been released the year before), the album also contains influences from bands like Judas Priest and, in addition to Van Halen-inspired guitar solos, features many speed metal-oriented guitar riffs.

I Am the Night (1985)

Release Date: August 16, 1985

I Am the Night is the third studio album by Pantera, released on August 16, 1985, through Metal Magic Records. It was the last Pantera album to feature lead singer Terry Glaze. I Am the Night was made available only on vinyl and cassette, with any subsequent CD releases being bootlegs transferred from the vinyl or tape originals. The track "Hot and Heavy" was released as a single and promoted with the band's second music video. The album sold 25,000+ copies, which was considered high for an independent release.

Power Metal (1988)

Release Date: June 24, 1988

Power Metal is the fourth studio album by Pantera, released in February 1988, through Metal Magic. The album signals a shift in a heavier direction and has been described as a "bridge" between the band's original glam metal style and the heavier groove metal sound of their subsequent work. Power Metal contains influences from glam ("Rock the World", "We’ll Meet Again", "Proud to be Loud", "Hard Ride",), heavy ("Over and Out", "Death Trap", "P.S.T. 88"), power ("Power Metal"), speed ("Power Metal", "Burnnn!","Down Below") and thrash metal ("Power Metal", "Over and Out", "Down Below", "Death Trap", "Burnnn!", "P.S.T. 88"), while still retaining sound from the band's two previous releases. The album's sound, particularly in Phil Anselmo's vocal performance, has been compared to that of Judas Priest. Guitarist Diamond Darrell performed lead vocals on "P*S*T*88".

Cowboys from Hell (1990)

Release Date: July 24, 1990

Cowboys from Hell is the fifth studio by Pantera, released on July 24, 1990, by Atlantic Records. It is the band's first commercial success, considered by many to be their true first studio album. It marks the beginning of a new chapter in their history and a change in image and sound, where they left glam rock / metal aside and began to exhibit traces of thrash metal , laying the foundations for what would be called groove metal, which materialized on the following album.

Vulgar Display of Power (1992)

Release Date: February 25, 1992

Vulgar Display of Power is the sixth studio album by Pantera. It marks groove metal as the band's definitive genre. During the 1990s, MTV's Headbangers Ball program used excerpts from songs on the album as opening, segment, and closing themes. Perhaps the most famous excerpt is Phil Anselmo's "hostile" scream, taken from the end of "Fucking Hostile". "Rise", "Regular People" and "Mouth for War" had versions recorded by game producer Robert Prince for the first-person shooter video game Doom, and a version of "This Love" appeared in Doom II. The album title comes from a line in the 1973 film The Exorcist. When Father Damien Karras suggests that Regan MacNeil (or the demon possessing her) break free from her bonds using her evil strength, Regan replies: "that's much too vulgar a display of power."

Far Beyond Driven (1994)

Release Date: March 22, 1994

Far Beyond Driven is the seventh studio album by Pantera, released in 1994. Immediately, it reached the top of the charts (#1 Billboard), being the first extreme metal band to achieve this feat. The album follows the trend of increasing aggression that Pantera's albums had been demonstrating. Phil Anselmo's personality becomes increasingly present in the band's style, presenting increasingly less elaborate, "raw" songs, and expressing facts from the personal lives of the band members. The extreme heaviness of this work stands out, no longer featuring any "heavy metal" vocals but rather the violent and deep timbre of thrash metal / hardcore. Far Beyond Driven is the product of Pantera's golden age, in which they toured with 200 shows a year. The album's original cover, which featured a drill inserted into a woman's (prostitute's) anus, was quickly replaced with one showing a drill piercing a human skull.

The Great Southern Trendkill (1996)

Release Date: May 7, 1996

The Great Southern Trendkill is the eighth studio album by Pantera, released in 1996. The Great Southern Trendkill was a reflection of the turbulent times Phil Anselmo was going through, filled with personal problems and drug use. These feelings are reflected in the songs, which are violent and full of hatred. It was considered the most violent album of the year, around the same time that Phil Anselmo suffered a near-fatal heroin overdose, during which he was considered dead for four minutes.

Reinventing the Steel (2000)

Release Date: March 21, 2000

Reinventing the Steel is the ninth and final studio album released by Pantera, launched in 2000. The song "Revolution Is My Name" received a Grammy Award nomination for Best Metal Performance in 2001. Most of the album's lyrics deal with aspects related to the band itself, such as in "We'll Grind That Axe for a Long Time" (where the band members recount how they remained "true" over the years, while many of their peers sold out for fame) and "I'll Cast a Shadow" (about Pantera's influence on the genre). There are also songs about the fans, such as "Goddamn Electric" (which mentions two of the band's influences, Black Sabbath and Slayer ) and "You've Got to Belong to It". The band dedicated Reinventing the Steel to their fans, whom they saw as their brothers.
